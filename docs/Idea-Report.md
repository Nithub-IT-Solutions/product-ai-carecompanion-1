## Original idea

Daisy is an always-on AI care companion for older adults who live alone. She stays with them day and night, like someone caring in the next room, and brings in family, a real nurse or emergency help when something is wrong.

The problem: about 15 million Americans aged 65+ live alone, and 63 million family caregivers try to look after them from a distance, spending about $7,200 a year of their own money. A home health aide costs about $78,000 a year, which most families cannot afford. Today’s options each solve one piece: medical-alert pendants only work when pressed (and most people don’t wear them), AI phone apps check in once a day but cannot act, and companion robots cannot call for help. Falls go unreported, medications get missed, blood pressure and diabetes drift, and loneliness grows.

What Daisy does:

Companion: warm daily conversation that remembers the person’s family, interests and stories, plus medication reminders with read-back confirmation.
Always-on monitoring: a plugged-in home hub is always on, so the person can just say “Daisy” from anywhere in the room. Over the first weeks Daisy learns their daily rhythm (waking, meals, medication, movement) and checks in when the day looks different. She also reads trends from blood-pressure cuffs, glucose monitors and smartwatch fall alerts.
Safety net: when something seems wrong, a tested rules engine (not the AI) runs a six-step escalation: a voice check-in, a countdown, alerts to family, then a staffed 24/7 monitoring center that calls emergency services.
Family bridge: a calm daily digest for the family, and when the person says they feel lonely, Daisy asks permission and prompts a family member to call.
Care logistics: appointment requests and rides booked after family approval.

Built on trust: Daisy always says she is an AI, never a nurse or doctor; real clinicians come from partner clinics. She never diagnoses or gives dose advice. She listens only after her name or a button press, has a privacy button, and the older person co-signs a care agreement that controls what family can see. Cameras are optional.

Why now: public payers have started funding AI companions (New York extended its program in October 2026; Washington Medicaid added coverage in March 2026), real-time voice AI now costs about $0.02 a minute, and Medicare’s 2026 remote-monitoring codes make clinic partnerships viable.

Business model: family plans from $24 to $89 a month, then contracts with aging agencies, Medicaid, Medicare Advantage plans, clinics and medical-alert companies. US first, then Canada, the UK and India, with the UAE and Qatar to follow.

Founder: Nisar Mohammad, 16 years in technology, leading AI and cloud programs at Capital One, Anthem and Parata. A working demo shows the full flow: conversation, a fall alert, family notification and monitoring-center escalation.

## Intended audience

Older adults aged 75+ who live alone and want to stay in their own homes, especially those managing high blood pressure or diabetes. The buyer is usually their adult child (45 to 65), who works and often lives in another city or country and wants peace of mind without daily phone duty. At scale, Daisy is also for organizations that care for older adults: aging agencies, Medicaid and Medicare Advantage plans, clinics, home-care providers and medical-alert companies.
